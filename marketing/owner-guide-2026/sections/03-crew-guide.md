# The Crew User Guide

This is what your people see when they pull out their phone on a job site. The portal was built to feel like an app, not a website. Big buttons, simple forms, two languages. If you can read this section, you can teach it in about ten minutes. Hand them the printout if it helps.

"Tap" means a finger on a phone screen. "Click" means a mouse. Ninety percent of crew use is on a phone, so this guide defaults to tap.

## First time logging in

Imagine the office just texted Diego his email and a 6-digit PIN. Here is what happens next.

1. He opens the portal URL on his phone — `bridgepointepainting.com/portal`. The first time, he saves this to his home screen. On iPhone: share button, then "Add to Home Screen." On Android: three-dot menu, then "Add to Home screen." From now on, the portal opens with one tap, like any other app.

2. He sees a dark login card with two fields: email and PIN. He types his work email and the 6-digit PIN the office gave him. Tap **Sign In**.

3. Because it is the first login, the portal stops him before the dashboard and asks him to pick a new PIN. He types the temporary PIN one more time, then a new one (4 to 12 digits — anything memorable), then types it again to confirm. Tap **Save**. From now on, only Diego knows his PIN.

4. If he prefers Spanish, the **EN | ES** toggle at the top of the login screen switches the whole portal — labels, buttons, error messages, headlines — in one tap. The portal remembers his choice on his phone.

> If a crew member forgets their PIN, they should not panic and should not try to guess. The office can reset it in under a minute. Same if they get locked out — eight wrong PIN attempts triggers a 15-minute cooldown. Tell them not to share PINs with each other. It is faster to get a reset than to type someone else's PIN by accident.

## What the dashboard shows

The dashboard is the first thing Diego sees after login and the page he lands on every time afterward.

At the top, a welcome card with his name and role. If he is clocked in, a green "On Shift" callout sits just below it, showing the time he clocked in and the project name. A **Clock Out** button is right inside that callout — he does not have to navigate anywhere to end his shift.

Under that, a row of stat tiles. For employees: **Active Jobs**, **Hours This Week**, **Open Tasks**, **Unread Messages**. For subs, three tiles — no "hours" tile, since subs bill instead of clocking.

Below the stats, a quick-action grid: **Clock In/Out**, **View Jobs**, **Photos**, **Updates**, **Pay** (or **Billings** for subs), **Messages**. Everything is also reachable from navigation, but most days the crew taps from here.

Then "Today's Jobs" — cards for any project the crew member is working on today. Below that, "Active Jobs" — every project they are currently assigned to. Tap a card to open the job. At the bottom, a recent activity feed shows the last few things that happened on their jobs.

## Navigation

On a phone, navigation lives at the bottom: four icons — **Dashboard**, **Jobs**, **Clock** (employees) or **Billings** (subs), and **Profile**. The full menu opens from the top-left hamburger button.

On a desktop or tablet, a sidebar runs down the left with three sections: **Main** (Dashboard, Jobs), **Time** (Clock, Timesheets, Pay for employees — just Billings for subs), and **Personal** (Profile, Documents, Messages). The current page is highlighted in gold. Worth pointing out once: the gold highlight always tells you where you are.

## The jobs list

Tap **Jobs** in the bottom nav or sidebar. Filter chips at the top: **All** / **Active** / **Upcoming** / **Completed**. Default is Active.

Each job is a card with project name, address, dates, and a progress bar. For employees with phases on a job, the card shows "X assigned to me" so they know what is theirs versus the rest of the crew's. For subs, the card shows the scope of their assignment and the agreed amount. Tap a card. The job page opens.

## Inside a job

A job page has three tabs: **Overview**, **Photos**, **Updates**.

**Overview** is the basic facts — address, dates, description, and a list of phases (sanding, prime, first coat, second coat, trim, cleanup, whatever the office set up). Each phase shows status (Not Started, In Progress, Completed, Blocked, Skipped) and percent complete.

**Photos** is a gallery of every photo ever uploaded to this job, plus a button to upload more. Photos are tagged Before / During / After / Issue / Other, and the gallery filters by tag.

**Updates** is a timeline. Every note, progress update, issue, and completion announcement, in date order, with the author's name.

### Marking a phase done

This is the action the office wants the crew doing at the end of every workday. About ten seconds.

From the **Overview** tab, tap any phase row. A modal pops up. At the top, three big quick-action buttons: **Mark Started** (blue), **Mark Blocked** (red), **Mark Completed** (green). For most days that is the entire interaction — tap **Mark Completed**, hit **Save**, the office sees it.

If the situation is in between — say, the second coat is partway done — a slider underneath runs from 0% to 100%. Drag to the right percentage. There is a notes field for context ("two more hours tomorrow morning") and an optional **Flag Issue** button if something is actually wrong on site.

Hit **Save**. The office sees the change immediately. There is no "send" button to remember and no email to compose. Saving is the whole communication.

> Tell the crew: end of every workday, open whatever phase you worked on, mark the new percentage, save. Thirty seconds. The office stops calling them, and they stop getting calls.

### Uploading photos

Tap the **Photos** tab inside a job, then tap **Upload photos**. The phone's native picker opens — the same one they use for texting a photo. They pick **Take Photo** to use the camera right now, or **Choose Library** to pick existing photos from their camera roll. They can pick multiple at once.

After picking, they see thumbnails of every photo about to upload. Above the thumbnails, two settings: a **tag** for the whole batch (Before / During / After / Issue / Other) and an optional **caption** for the batch. Each individual photo also has its own caption field if one needs more detail.

Tap **Upload N photos**. The portal compresses each photo on the phone before sending, which is critical because most jobsites have lousy signal. A 12 MB iPhone photo gets shrunk to about 400 KB before it leaves the device. The progress bar fills up at the bottom. When it hits 100%, the photos are in the gallery.

> Rule of thumb for the crew: one **before** photo when you start a phase. One or two **during** photos at meaningful milestones — fresh sanding, first coat done, that kind of thing. Two or three **after** photos when the work is signed off. That's it. The office uses these to handle disputes and build the marketing portfolio. Crew member doesn't have to think about it — just tag and shoot.

### Posting an update

From inside a job, tap the **Updates** tab, then the compose box. Type whatever happened. Underneath, four chips: **Note** / **Progress** / **Issue** / **Completion**. Tap the right one. Tap **Post update**. Done.

The good ones are short and concrete:

- "Cabinets dry, ready for second coat tomorrow." (Note)
- "First coat finished on the great room and dining room." (Progress)
- "Hairline crack found on the north wall behind the dresser. Need a call before we paint over it." (Issue)
- "Punch list complete. Walking the homeowner through it at 4." (Completion)

When to post: end of workday, a quick Note. Anything blocking work, an Issue — that one flags in the office immediately. Phase wrapped, a Completion. Updates replace the group text and the late-night call to Patrick. Once the crew gets in the habit, the office stops chasing.

## Clocking in (employees only — subs skip this section)

Subcontractors bill against an agreed amount, so they do not clock in or out. Employees, this is the most-used screen in the portal.

Tap **Clock** in the bottom nav. The page is intentionally simple — one huge button in the center. If not on shift, the button is gold and says **Clock In**. If already on shift, the screen shows elapsed time as a giant counter and the button at the bottom says **Clock Out**.

To clock in: pick a project from the dropdown. If the project has phases, an optional second dropdown lets them pick which one. They can type a quick note — also optional. Tap **Clock In**.

The screen flips to live timer mode. They can leave the page, lock their phone, drive across town — the timer keeps running on the server. At the end of the day, tap **Clock Out**, confirm the duration in the popup ("Clock out after 7:15?"), and the portal calculates regular and overtime hours. Overtime kicks in after 8 hours on a single shift.

Hours show up as **Pending** on their timesheet and on the office side. The office reviews and approves them once a week, usually Friday. Once approved, the status flips to **Approved** and the entry is locked.

The portal remembers the last project and phase used, so the next morning the dropdowns are already filled in.

### Manual entries

Sometimes a crew member forgets to clock in. Truck breaks down, brain fog, kid woke up sick. Instead of texting Brenda, they go to the **Timesheets** page and tap **Add manual entry**. Pick the date, the project, hours regular plus overtime. Write a short note ("forgot to clock in — was on the Buckhead exterior 7 to 3:30"). Save. The office reviews and either approves it or asks a question. Missed clock-ins become a 30-second form, not a missed paycheck.

## My pay (employees) and My billings (subs)

### Employees: My pay

Tap **Pay** in the sidebar. Three tabs: **This Week**, **Last Week**, **Year to Date**. Each row is a worked day with regular hours, overtime hours, and gross pay. Status pills show **Pending** (waiting for office approval) or **Approved**.

Read-only. They cannot edit pay records from here. If a number looks wrong, the right move is usually a manual entry on Timesheets rather than arguing about a finalized record.

### Subs: My billings

Tap **Billings** in the sidebar or bottom nav. Totals across the top: **Agreed across all projects**, **Billed to date**, **Paid to date**, **Unpaid balance**. Below that, a list of every assignment with scope, project, completion percentage, billed amount, paid amount, and balance.

Subs can see exactly what they're owed and what's coming, without calling the office. Also read-only. To submit a new invoice they still go through the office. The portal is for visibility, not invoicing.

## Documents

Tap **Documents** in the sidebar. The page shows every document tied to that crew member.

For employees: W-4, I-9, OSHA cards, driver's license, anything else the office requires. Each row shows document name, upload date, and verification status (Verified / Pending / Expired).

For subs: W-9, Certificate of Insurance, Master Service Agreement. The COI gets a yellow warning thirty days before it expires and a red banner once it actually has — that prompt to renew used to be Patrick's job to remember.

To upload, tap the gold **Upload document** button. The portal asks for the type, issue date, expiry date if applicable, and a URL — Dropbox link, Google Drive share link, or any public-ish file URL. The office gets a notification, verifies, and flips the status.

URL-based on purpose. Crew members don't need to know how to scan or PDF anything from a phone. They photograph the document, upload to their cloud of choice, paste the link, done.

## Messages

Tap **Messages**. The office's announcement channel — one-way for now.

Each message has a level. **Info** messages are gray ("Office closed Monday for the holiday"). **Important** messages have a gold border ("Updated handling instructions for the new Sherwin-Williams primer"). **Urgent** messages are red ("All hands meeting tomorrow 7 AM"). Unread messages have a gold left border.

Tap a message to read it in full. The unread counter on the dashboard ticks down as messages get read. **Mark all read** clears the counter at the end of the week.

This is not chat. It is the modern equivalent of the cork board in the office, except every crew member gets it on their phone. If a crew member needs to actually message Patrick, they still call or text the way they always have.

## Language toggle

Bilingual is built into every page, not bolted on. The toggle lives at the bottom of the sidebar on desktop and inside the menu drawer on mobile. **EN | ES**. Tap it. The whole portal switches in one second. Sidebar labels, button text, error messages, page titles, every form field, every status. The choice is remembered on that device.

A Spanish-first crew member can use the entire portal — clock in, upload photos, post updates, change PIN, view pay — without seeing English at all. Same data. Only the labels change.

## Profile and changing PIN

Tap **Profile** in the sidebar or the bottom nav.

For employees, the editable fields are: phone, address, emergency contact name, emergency contact phone. Name and email are locked — those changes go through the office. For subs: company contact name, company phone, company email, address.

Below the form, a **Change PIN** card. Tap it. A modal opens with three fields: current PIN, new PIN, confirm new PIN. PIN must be 4 to 12 digits. Tap **Save**. Done. Next login uses the new PIN.

> Tell the crew to change their PIN if they ever feel weird about someone seeing them type it. Five seconds, no awkward conversation.

## Tips for the field

A few things to mention in person when introducing the portal:

- **Save the URL to the home screen.** One tap to open. Treat it like an app.
- **Bad signal is fine.** Photos compress on the phone before uploading, and the app retries when service comes back. Most jobsite uploads work on one bar.
- **Eight wrong PINs locks the account for 15 minutes.** Wait it out, or call the office for a reset. Faster to ask.
- **Don't share PINs.** Even with crew they trust. The office issues a fresh PIN faster than typing someone else's anyway.
- **Subs: bookmark the Billings page.** Before calling about a payment, check there first. Most questions are already answered.
- **End of every workday: post a Note and mark phase progress.** Two taps. The office stops chasing, and they stop getting Friday-night calls.
- **Issue updates flag in the office instantly.** Use them for anything blocking work, not general grumbling.
- **Photos are forever.** Even if a crew member's phone breaks or gets sold, the portal uploads are safe. That alone is worth showing them.

The portal was built to make their day easier, not harder. The first week feels like a change. The second week, they stop noticing it. By the third, they wonder how anyone managed before.
